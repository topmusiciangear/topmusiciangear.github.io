Best Stream Deck Alternatives & Streaming Mixers

Streaming sound is 90% routing, and the gear below turns that routing into something you can actually grab with your hands. I spent weeks swapping the Stream Deck+, the Streamer X, the BEACN Mix Create, the Wave XLR in and out of my live setup so you don't have to guess.

By Daniel Carnago · August 2026

Touring and session musician with 20+ years on stages around the world. A great deal of the gear I recommend here I have used myself, on tours, in professional studios, or in my own studio.

How I test gear: to be honest, I have not tested every product on this site. Much of it I have used myself in live shows and recording sessions; others I have seen tested by musicians and sound techs I trust; and the rest I have researched in depth before recommending.
Verdict
The Stream Deck+ is the total control pick with buttons, dials and a touch strip for every stream setup. The RØDECaster Pro II is hardware reliability for audio-focused streamers, and the BEACN Mix Create adds clever software routing. Match the tool to how you stream.

How to Choose a Streaming Mixer and Controller

A streaming mixer is the physical brain of your stream audio. It keeps your mic, your game, your music and your chat on separate channels so viewers hear a clean mix instead of one giant muddled blob. Most people start with cheap software routing and buy a real unit the first time something goes wrong mid-stream.

First, pick the shape. Do you want a controller that drives your existing software, a dedicated mixer that actually processes audio, or an all-in-one box? The Elgato Stream Deck+ ($160) is the best-selling controller, period — it sits next to whatever you already own and gives you buttons and dials for everything. The BEACN Mix Create ($229) is the software-first mixer that routes every app on your PC through a 5-inch screen and four knobs. The Streamer X ($229) throws an interface and a 4K capture card into the same compact box. And the Wave XLR ($170) is just a really good mic interface that plugs into the Elgato world.

Now check the software, because that is what you will live with. All four run through desktop apps, and that app shapes your whole workflow. The one you actually enjoy using every single day is the one you will keep on your desk.

Is the Elgato Stream Deck+ the Best Streaming Controller?

The Elgato Stream Deck+ ($160) is the controller that keeps selling out for a reason. Right now it sits as the #1 Best Seller in digital audio workstation controllers, and once you spend an afternoon with it, the why is obvious. Eight LCD keys, four haptic dials and a thin touch strip sit under your fingers, and each one can trigger anything — switching scenes, toggling your mic, dimming your lights, jumping between Spotify playlists.

Where it shines is the plugins. The Wave Link plugin alone turns the deck into a full audio surface: mute channels, flip effects, swap outputs, all without touching the mouse. OBS, Twitch, Discord, smart home — there are hundreds of plugins, and multi-action support fires a whole chain with one press. Set up a smart profile and it even reconfigures itself when you tab between apps.

The honest catch: it controls software, it does not mix audio. It has no XLR input and no preamp. Think of it as the conductor's baton, not the orchestra — pair it with a Wave XLR or any interface and you have a complete desk.

Is the Elgato Stream Deck + XL Worth It? (36 Keys, Six Dials)

If your stream has outgrown the eight keys of the Stream Deck+, the + XL is the grown-up version of the same idea. You get 36 LCD keys in an 8x4 grid plus a row of six tactile dials and a touch strip, which means your whole production — scenes, cameras, mics, sound effects, chat, lighting, even your outro — can live on a single page with nothing tucked behind folders. The dials work as 360-degree encoders with a push action for mutes or toggles, so you can ride a fader in Wave Link and tap to mute it without reaching for the mouse.

For who? Streamers running OBS with lots of scenes, podcasters juggling multiple tools at once, and anyone producing video where every second counts. If you're still using a keyboard shortcut to switch scenes or you're constantly opening folders to find an action, the + XL pays for itself in saved fumbling.

The honest trade-offs. It's $350, so it's not an impulse buy, and you have to be honest about whether you'll actually fill 36 keys. If you only need a handful of shortcuts, the Stream Deck+ at $160 gives you most of the benefit for less money. But if you've ever wished for more buttons and real dials, the + XL is the last controller you'll need to buy.

Is the Rode Streamer X worth it? (The Software Issue Honestly Explained)

The Rode Streamer X ($229) is the most clever space-saver on this list. It squeezes an audio interface, a 4K capture card and a little control surface into one compact box, so console streamers and dual-PC folks can ditch three separate pieces of gear. The Revolution preamp pushes 76dB of gain, which is honestly a lot — a Shure SM7B or an EV RE20 sounds full and loud with zero boosters in the chain. Video side, you get 4K30 capture with 4K60 passthrough and VRR support, plus four SMART pads for sounds and voice FX.

Now the part I have to be straight with you about: the software. The hardware is great; the UNIFY app is where the complaints live, and they are fair. It can be unstable upon reboots and requires precise power-cycling — wake your PC from sleep and the virtual mixer sometimes loses the audio route, leaving your mic silent until you close and reopen the app. That is exactly what you will find in the user reviews if you go digging, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

Who should buy it? If you stream from a console or a single PC and want one tidy box that handles audio and video, it is hard to beat for the money. If your setup is a complex dual-PC machine and you need software that never flinches, the Elgato or BEACN ecosystems are the safer bet.

Is the BEACN Mix Create the Best USB-C Streaming Mixer?

The BEACN Mix Create comes from the same team that built the GoXLR, and it pushes the software-first approach to its logical end. Instead of motorized faders, you get four infinite-scroll knobs, a 5-inch color screen and the BEACN app, which turns every application, game, mic, headset and audio interface on your PC into a channel you can grab. Nearly 330 Buyers rate it 4.3/5, and streamers keep calling it the first audio controller that genuinely improves a setup.

The core trick is the dual-mix system. You get a Personal Mix for your ears and an Audience Mix for the stream, and you can unlink them so the game is loud in your headphones while viewers hear a balanced broadcast — no OBS rebuilding required. Knob paging means four physical knobs control an unlimited number of channels: page one for game, chat, music and system, page two for your browser, mic, soundboard and VOD. Up to three knobs can be locked so your most-used channels are always visible.

There is also a dedicated VOD track. It strips copyrighted music out of your archived recording while keeping it live for your audience, which is the cleanest DMCA workaround I have used on a sub-$300 device. The routing table in the app is a simple grid that replaces virtual-cable spaghetti: click where a source goes and it goes there.

The honest trade-offs: there is no XLR input — this is a software controller, not an audio interface, so for a professional XLR mic you still need an interface like the BEACN Studio or the Wave XLR MK.2. It is fully dependent on the BEACN app, which can be finicky after updates and occasionally fails to recognize the device until you restart it. And the all-plastic build at $229 feels less premium than the price suggests.

Who should buy it? If you run a USB mic or a gaming headset and want broadcast-grade routing without rebuilding your whole setup in OBS, the Mix Create is the most flexible unit here. If you need XLR inputs, pair it with the BEACN Studio or look at the Wave XLR MK.2.

Is the Elgato Wave XLR MK.2 the Best Mic Interface for Streamers?

The Elgato Wave XLR MK.2 ($170) is the quiet workhorse of the Elgato family. It takes any XLR microphone and makes it sound broadcast-ready. The headline number is 80dB of ultra-low-noise gain — that is enough clean power to drive an SM7B without any booster sitting between the mic and the box. You also get 24-bit/48kHz conversion, tap-to-mute, and a dial that flips between gain, voice tune, headphone volume and the mic/PC mix.

What makes the MK.2 special is the Wave FX Processor. Built with LEWITT Audio, it runs five onboard DSP effects — low-cut, expander, voice tune, compressor and EQ — plus VST plugins, all on the hardware with zero-latency monitoring. Your processed voice sounds the same in every app because the processing follows the mic itself, not the software. No virtual cables, no fiddly routing.

Clipguard 2.0 is a small blessing. With 135dB of dynamic range, loud peaks get caught before they ever distort, so you can stop babysitting your gain and just talk. The honest limitation is that it is an interface, not a controller — you still want a Stream Deck alongside it to actually run your stream.

Conclusion

If you want your hands on everything, get the Stream Deck+ ($160) — nothing else makes your whole stream so instantly controllable. If you want hands-on control over every audio source on your PC, the BEACN Mix Create ($229) is the software-first pick. If you stream from a console and want one compact box that does audio and video, the Streamer X ($229) is the value pick. And if you just need your mic to sound incredible, the Wave XLR MK.2 ($170) delivers. There is genuinely no wrong answer here — it comes down to which workflow feels like yours.
