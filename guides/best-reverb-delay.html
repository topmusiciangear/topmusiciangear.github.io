Best Reverb & Delay Pedals for Guitar

Reverb and delay are the core time-based effects. Extensively tested in both studio and live settings, every pick earns its place.

By Daniel Carnago · August 2026

Touring and session musician with 20+ years on stages around the world. A great deal of the gear I recommend here I have used myself, on tours, in professional studios, or in my own studio.

Verdict
The DD-8 is the versatile delay that does everything from slap to shimmer. The Hall of Fame 2 is the value reverb with great algorithms, and the BigSky MX is the pro reverb for spaces that sound like a real room.
Boss DD-8 Digital Delay
Pros
  • 11 delay modes in compact box
  • Looper (40 sec), tap tempo, carryover
  • Stereo I/O, MIDI, expression pedal
  • Stereo ins/outs with tap tempo and 40-second looper
Cons
  • Menu diving for deep params
  • No dedicated knobs per param
  • Looper basic vs dedicated
  • Tiny mode knob shifts easily if bumped on crowded boards
TC Electronic Hall of Fame 2
Pros
  • 10 reverb algorithms, TonePrint
  • MASH footswitch = expressive control
  • Stereo I/O, true bypass option
  • TonePrint slots plus Mash expression footswitch
Cons
  • No dedicated delay section
  • Editor required for deep editing
  • Single footswitch limited
  • MASH pressure switch takes practice to control evenly
Strymon BigSky MX
Pros
  • 12 reverb machines, studio quality
  • MIDI, 300 presets, stereo I/O
  • New: cabinet modeling, freeze enhancements
  • The reverb pedal benchmark
Cons
  • Expensive
  • Large footprint
  • Complex menu system
  • Needs isolated high-current power, no daisy-chaining
Strymon TimeLine
Pros
  • 12 delay machines, 200 presets
  • Looper (30 sec), MIDI, stereo I/O
  • Filter, grit, modulation per machine
  • Delay gold standard
Cons
  • Very expensive
  • Large, complex
  • No dedicated tap tempo footswitch
  • 30-second looper falls short for longer song structures
Meris LVX
Pros
  • Structures: modular reverb+delay
  • 8 simultaneous engines, stereo
  • MIDI, 99 presets, expression control
  • Unique sound design tool
Cons
  • Steep learning curve
  • No dedicated knobs per param
  • Expensive
  • High current draw rules out daisy-chain power supplies
Chase Bliss Habit
Pros
  • Records everything, scan/loop past
  • Experimental delay/looper hybrid
  • MIDI, dip switches, expression
  • Inspires happy accidents
Cons
  • Very niche workflow
  • Expensive
  • Can be confusing live
  • No screen, so finding saved moments relies on memory
Eventide TimeFactor
Pros
  • 10 delay types, vintage + modern
  • Looper (12 sec), MIDI, stereo
  • Modulation, filtering per algorithm
  • Classic rack sound in pedal
Cons
  • Older platform, larger
  • No USB, limited preset management
  • High current draw (400 mA)
  • Bank changes mid-song demand careful two-switch footwork
Source Audio Nemesis
Pros
  • 26 delay engines, full MIDI, 128 presets, Neuro app
  • Stereo I/O, MIDI, expression
  • Compact, 128 presets onboard
  • Best value for tweakers
Cons
  • App required for deep editing
  • Small knobs, tight spacing
  • No dedicated looper
  • Scrolling presets without the app means tapping banks blind
Keeley Caverns V2
Pros
  • Magnetic tape-style delay with wow/flutter modulation up to 650 ms
  • Spring, Shimmer and Modulated reverbs switch independently
  • Trails or true bypass with top-mounted jacks
  • Two effects for $199 in a compact box
Cons
  • Mono in/out only — no stereo spread
  • No presets, MIDI or deep editing
  • Digital emulation, not real tape or springs
  • 650 ms maximum is short next to long digital delays
UA Del-Verb
Pros
  • Starlight tape/Memory Man plus Golden spring/plate/hall in one box
  • Dual stereo engines you can combine
  • Tap tempo plus downloadable voicings via app
  • Analog dry-through with silent switching
Cons
  • PSU sold separately and it needs 400 mA isolated
  • Deep control lives in the app, not on the panel
  • $349 buys six sounds — pricey per effect
  • No 5-pin MIDI DIN — MIDI only over USB/Bluetooth
MXR M169 Carbon Copy
Pros
  • 100% analog bucket-brigade warmth
  • 600 ms with switchable modulation
  • 26 mA sips power — runs on a 9V battery
  • Three knobs and done
Cons
  • Dark repeats lack digital clarity
  • No presets, MIDI, tap tempo or display
  • Modulation trims live inside on tiny pots
  • 600 ms maximum is short for ambient washes
UA Golden Reverberator
Pros
  • Spring 65, Plate 140 and Hall 224 verbs
  • Analog dry-through in all modes
  • True or buffered bypass with trails
  • Stereo and dual-mono operation
Cons
  • PSU sold separately and it needs 400 mA isolated
  • Deep editing needs the UAFX app
  • $399 for three verbs — flagship pricing
  • No shimmer/reverse weirdness — classics only
Boss RV-200
Pros
  • 12 verbs including Arpverb plus 127 memories
  • Studio spec: 32-bit AD/DA at 96 kHz
  • MIDI, expression and USB control
  • Runs on 3xAA batteries or adapter
Cons
  • Deep functions hide behind the Param knob
  • Mini-TRS MIDI and micro-B USB need adapters
  • 260 mA eats batteries fast
  • No XLR for direct PA use

How to Choose the Best Reverb or Delay Pedal?

The best time-based pedal depends on whether you want ambiance or rhythmic repeats. The Boss DD-8 is the most versatile delay with 11 modes from analog to shimmer. The TC Electronic Hall of Fame 2 covers every reverb need with its TonePrint feature. The Strymon BigSky MX is the high-end reverb with 16 machines and dynamic reverb. Consider whether consider a dedicated pedal or a multi-effects solution.

Bonus Looping: RC-5 Loop Station

The Boss RC-5 Loop Station is the most powerful compact looper for practice and live use. It records up to 13 hours of stereo audio with 99 phrase memories, and its 57 rhythm patterns with 7 drum kits turn any pedal into a full backing band. TRS MIDI I/O syncs it with any rig. Pair it with the DD-8 or Hall of Fame 2 for a complete time-based setup.

Reverb first for most players. Reverb is the foundation of your sound — it adds space and depth to everything you play. Delay is a creative, rhythmic effect that comes second. That said, many players buy both together. If you can only start with one, get a versatile reverb like the Hall of Fame 2; the Boss DD-8 delay is the perfect second pedal.
Set the delay time to match the song tempo, keep the mix low (15-25%), and use repeats sparingly. The Boss DD-8 makes this easy with its tap tempo. For a clean slapback echo, use one short repeat; for ambient washes, crank the repeats and add the shimmer mode. The key is subtlety — delay should add space, not clutter.

Conclusion

The Boss DD-8 is the most versatile delay for any guitarist. The TC Electronic Hall of Fame 2 offers great reverb value with TonePrint customization. The Strymon BigSky MX is the reverb professionals reach for who demand the best. Start with the DD-8 and Hall of Fame 2 for a complete time-based setup. Hybrid hunters should hear the Keeley Caverns V2 and UA Del-Verb, analog purists get the MXR M169 Carbon Copy, the UA Golden Reverberator nails classic studio verbs, and the Boss RV-200 packs presets and 32-bit sound in a compact box.
