DT 770 Pro vs DT 990 Pro: Closed or Open for Mixing?

The Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro and DT 990 Pro are legendary German headphones that every producer debates.

By Daniel Carnago · August 2026

Touring and session musician with 20+ years on stages around the world. A great deal of the gear I recommend here I have used myself, on tours, in professional studios, or in my own studio.

How I test gear: to be honest, I have not tested every product on this site. Much of it I have used myself in live shows and recording sessions; others I have seen tested by musicians and sound techs I trust; and the rest I have researched in depth before recommending.
Verdict
The DT 770 Pro is your tracking headphone — closed, comfortable, isolating. The DT 990 Pro is the mixing pair with open detail. If you record and mix, both is the move.
Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro
Pros
  • Closed-back design with velour earpads provides exceptional passive isolation up to 35dB, letting you track vocals or record in the same room as a drum kit without bleed
  • Bass response extends down to 5Hz and is elevated around 80Hz, giving kick drums and bass lines a powerful punchy feel that makes tracking low-end satisfying
  • Every single part is replaceable — headband padding earpads cable and driver assembly are all user-serviceable with standard tools
  • Steel headband with robust ABS plastic construction withstands being tossed in gear bags and daily studio abuse for years
  • Detachable coiled cable standard on the 80-ohm version gives 3 meters of reach with retracting coils and no cable noise transmitted to earcups
Cons
  • Elevated bass response can mislead mixing decisions — tracks balanced on DT 770 may translate as bass-light on flat monitors
  • Notorious 8kHz spike adds artificial sibilance and harshness on cymbals and vocal sibilants, causing ear fatigue during long sessions
  • Non-detachable cable on 32-ohm and 250-ohm models means cable failure requires soldering repair
  • High clamping force causes discomfort during sessions longer than two hours for users with wider head shapes
Beyerdynamic DT 990 Pro
Pros
  • Open-back design creates a natural spacious soundstage with clear air and separation between instruments, critical for mixing decisions where spatial positioning matters
  • Treble emphasis around 9kHz gives transients and high-frequency details a crisp articulate edge revealing flaws in reverb tails and cymbal decay
  • Lightweight at 290 grams with plush velour earpads that barely compress over time, comfortable for 6-8 hour mixing sessions
  • Bass is tight and controlled rather than boomy, giving accurate sub-bass extension and low-end punch without masking midrange clarity
  • Same user-serviceable modular design as DT 770 — every component from cable to driver can be replaced at home
Cons
  • Open-back offers virtually no isolation — bleed from room noise leaks in and headphones leak sound audibly to others nearby
  • Pronounced 9kHz treble peak causes listening fatigue after 2-3 hours and mixes may sound dull on systems lacking that high-frequency emphasis
  • No removable cable on any version — cable break requires soldering repair on the entire headphone
  • Bass lacks sub-bass extension below 30Hz that electronic music producers need to feel low-end content

Is the DT 770 Pro the Best for Tracking and Isolation?

The DT 770 Pro (closed-back) is the industry standard for tracking — recording vocals, instruments, and overdubs where you need isolation. The closed-back design keeps sound from bleeding into your microphone and blocks out room noise. The bass boost (+5dB at 70Hz) helps you feel the groove during takes. Comfortable for hours with velour earpads. If you record with microphones in the same room, get the DT 770.

Is the DT 990 Pro the Best for Mixing and Soundstage?

The DT 990 Pro (open-back) excels at mixing and critical listening. The open-back design creates a wide, airy soundstage that rivals studio monitors. You hear natural stereo separation, reverb tails, and depth that closed-back headphones simply can't reproduce. The treble emphasis (+6dB at 10kHz) reveals detail and sibilance issues in your mix — but can be fatiguing for long sessions. If you mix and master, get the DT 990.

Do You Need a Headphone Amp for the DT 770 or DT 990?

Yes, the DT 990 Pro needs a headphone amplifier — its 250-ohm impedance requires more power than a laptop or phone can provide. The DT 770 Pro's 80-ohm version is far more forgiving and works straight from most interfaces. Plug the 250-ohm DT 990 directly into a laptop or phone and you'll get quiet, thin sound. The 80-ohm DT 770, on the other hand, is loud and clear from a Scarlett 2i2, SSL 2+, or any interface headphone out. For the DT 990, add a dedicated headphone amp like the JDS Labs Atom or Schiit Magni for the best performance. With proper amplification, both headphones transform from good to exceptional.

Verdict: Which Should You Buy?

The honest answer most engineers will give: buy BOTH. The DT 770 Pro for tracking and the DT 990 Pro for mixing. If you can only buy one, your use case decides: if you record with mics in the same room, the DT 770 Pro is non-negotiable for bleed prevention. If you only produce electronic music with no live recording, the DT 990 Pro's soundstage makes mixing faster and more accurate. Skip whichever compromises your workflow less — then save up for the other one.

Conclusion

The DT 770 Pro and DT 990 Pro are the Batman and Superman of studio headphones — different but both essential. The DT 770 Pro gives you isolation for tracking and a satisfying bass response. The DT 990 Pro rewards you with breathtaking soundstage for mixing. At their price points (-), no other headphones offer this combination of German build quality, comfort, and performance. The only wrong answer is buying neither.
