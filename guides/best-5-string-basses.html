Why Five Strings, and How to Choose Your Tier

A low B only works if the whole bass supports it: scale length, bridge coupling, nut width and preamp headroom. Cheap 5-string basses fail exactly there — floppy B, muddy definition. This guide splits twelve verified basses into three honest tiers so you pay for support, not logos.

Budget tier proves the concept: Yamaha build quality, StingRay thump and Fender voice with compromises you can hear around the edges. Mid-range is where working bassists live — pro components, stable tuning, session-ready tone. Premium buys definitive low B: multiscale geometry, neodymium magnets and 18-volt headroom.

Buying Advice: What Actually Holds a Low B Together

Scale length first. Thirty-four inches works with the right bridge and setup; fan designs (33–35 in on the EHB1005MS, 34–37 in on the Combustion) tune each string to its ideal tension, which is why multiscale B-strings sound like piano wire. Through-body stringing (BB435, AmPro II) adds a second layer of sustain.

Headroom second. High-headroom active preamps (18V designs, Gen 2 Sire heritage) stay clean where 9-volt circuits clip — that is clean punch, not more volume. Passive basses (BB435, CV 70s, Affinity) skip the issue entirely with zero batteries.

Fit third. Nut widths here run 43 to 47.6 mm and spacing 16.5 to 19 mm: narrow and tight for small hands (GSR205B, SR505A), wide and roomy for slap (Affinity, CV 70s, AmPro II). For starting points by budget see beginner bass guitars and best basses under $700.
