Best Overdrive & Distortion Pedals for Guitar

Overdrive and distortion pedals are the backbone of electric guitar tone. Years of stage use from clubs to festivals back every pick.

By Daniel Carnago · October 2026

Touring and session musician with 20+ years on stages around the world. A great deal of the gear I recommend here I have used myself, on tours, in professional studios, or in my own studio.

Verdict

Boss BD-2 Blues Driver is a step ahead of the rest. The most musical overdrive pedal ever made. From subtle grit to full saturation, the BD-2 responds to your playing dynamics like no other pedal.

How to Choose the Best Overdrive or Distortion Pedal?

The right overdrive or distortion pedal depends on your amp and style. The Ibanez TS9 is the classic tube screamer that pushes your amp into sweet saturation. The Boss BD-2 offers responsive, touch-sensitive overdrive that cleans up when you roll back your volume. The ProCo RAT 2 goes from gritty overdrive to searing distortion with one twist. Think about whether you want to push your amp (overdrive) or be your amp's primary gain (distortion).

The Mid-Hump Classic: TS9 Tube Screamer

If your solos disappear in the band mix, this green classic fixes that like nothing else. Its famous mid push trims flabby lows and lifts the frequencies where guitar lives, so a tube amp sings with smooth, vocal-like sustain instead of fizzy gain. The three-knob layout is gloriously simple: set output high and gain low to push an already cooking amp. The pedal tightens rhythm tones and lifts leads with the same box just by riding guitar volume. Essential for blues and rock; check that you want mid-forward color rather than transparent boost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Yes, the Ibanez TS9 is the most recorded overdrive in history — its mid-frequency bump pushes tube amps into sweet saturation.
The Boss BD-2 uses asymmetrical clipping that preserves note definition at any gain setting — responsive and touch-sensitive like no other.
The ProCo RAT 2 defined alternative rock with its LM308 chip — from gritty overdrive to searing distortion at a hard-to-beat price.
Overdrive is a soft, tube-like saturation that cleans up when you roll back your guitar volume — the TS9 and BD-2 are overdrives. Distortion is harder, more compressed clipping that stays heavy even at lower gain — the ProCo RAT 2 is a distortion. Overdrive pushes an already-amp-like tone; distortion shapes the tone itself. Many players use an overdrive to boost a distortion, or a distortion for the heavier sounds.
Choose the TS9 if you play blues, classic rock, or want a mid-boost to cut through a mix — it is the SRV/Mayer-style overdrive. Choose the RAT if you play alternative, grunge, punk, or hard rock and want a fuzzy, aggressive distortion that is still musical. They are very different: the TS9 pushes, the RAT shapes. Many boards run both, with the TS9 boosting the RAT for solos.
The BD-2 handles medium-high gain well, but it is not a metal distortion. Its asymmetrical clipping gives a natural, dynamic breakup that works great for rock, blues, and crunchy tones, and it cleans up beautifully with the guitar volume. For heavier, saturated high-gain, add a dedicated distortion or boost the BD-2 with the TS9. For most classic rock and blues, the BD-2 covers it on its own.

Conclusion

Boss BD-2 Blues Driver is still the reference in the category. From subtle grit to full saturation, the BD-2 responds to your playing dynamics like no other pedal.
